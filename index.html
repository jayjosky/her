About us

A refugee-led organization building self-reliance in Kalobeyei

Help Empower Refugees (HER) was founded in 2024 by refugees and formally registered in 2026 in Turkana County, Kenya. We equip young people with education, practical skills and livelihood opportunities to build self-reliance, strengthen their families and drive positive change in their communities.

Our vision

A world where everyone is empowered to live a better, self-reliant life.

Our mission

To empower women, youth and families through education, skills development, entrepreneurship and sustainable livelihood opportunities that foster self-reliance and resilient communities.

Where we work

Kalobeyei & Kakuma, Turkana County

HER primarily serves refugees and asylum seekers in the Kalobeyei Integrated Settlement and Kakuma Refugee Camp, with selected programs also engaging surrounding host communities in Turkana County.

Leadership: James Ladu Sabastiano, Founder and Director.
Programs

Skills that build income, confidence and community

Every HER program is designed with the community it serves — practical, locally run and focused on lasting self-reliance.

Livelihoods

Poultry farming & climate-smart agriculture

Our poultry project is run by young people trained in every stage of production — brooding day-old chicks under a warm, well-managed brooder ring, feeding and vaccination routines, biosecurity, and record keeping through to market-ready birds.

The flock provides protein and income for participating households, and the enterprise doubles as a hands-on training ground for the next cohort of youth farmers.

Skills & enterprise

Digital skills & media training

Young people learn photography, videography, editing and computer literacy — practical digital skills they can turn into freelance work, documentation jobs and small media businesses.

Trainees document HER's own programs and community events, so the story of Kalobeyei is told by the people who live it.

Community

Community engagement, education & wellbeing

We run outreach and community engagement activities, food and essential support distributions, early childhood development, community education, SRHR awareness and climate awareness sessions.

These activities create safe, joyful spaces for children and youth, and bring refugee and host community families together.
